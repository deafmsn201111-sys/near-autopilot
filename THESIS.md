# Investment thesis: on NEAR DeFi, TVL and the displayed APR do not tell you the yield you keep

> Generated by `pnpm thesis:rebuild` from the raw chain data in `thesis/data/raw/`. Do not edit by hand: a test
> fails if this file differs from what the rebuild produces. Every number below can be recomputed offline from the
> committed raw files, and every raw point names the mainnet block it was read at.

**Verdict: SUPPORTED on all three counts.** Data: Rhea v1 and Burrow on NEAR mainnet, 91 daily blocks (90 days) from
2026-08-06 (block 210237960) to 2026-11-04 (block 223087984).

## The claim

TVL and nominal APR alone do not show the real, sustainable net yield of NEAR DeFi markets. An investment decision
needs net yield after impermanent loss and costs, liquidity, data quality and risk. Tested as three counts, with the
rules that would falsify each fixed in advance:

| | Count | Falsified if | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | The 24h fee APR a pool list shows is not what an LP kept | nominal is within 2 pp of LP vs HODL in at least half the pools | 4 of 15 within 2 pp: **holds** |
| H2 | TVL does not rank pools by what LPs kept | TVL rank predicts LP vs HODL with \|Spearman ρ\| ≥ 0.7 | ρ = 0.22: **holds** |
| H3 | Burrow's displayed supply APR is not the yield accrued | displayed is within 0.5 pp of realized in at least 3/4 of markets | 11 of 18 within: **holds** |

## Evidence

- **Universe.** v1 SIMPLE_POOLs with wrap.near on one side, ranked by wrap.near reserve at block 223087984, top 15; 5162 candidate pools, 15 analysed.
- **Nominal above kept.** In **13 of 15** pools the nominal 24h APR on the last day is above the
  LP-vs-HODL return of the whole window. Median nominal 0.9%,
  median realized fee APR 0.7%, median LP vs HODL -2.2%.
- **The displayed number moves.** Over the window the daily nominal APR of a pool spans a median
  13.4 pp between its lowest and highest day.
- **Impermanent loss decides.** 12 of 15 pools left their LPs behind simply holding the two tokens
  (LP vs HODL < 0): fees did not cover the impermanent loss.
- **Size matters.** After round-trip costs (two half-swaps through the pool, gas upper bound), the net APR of a $500
  position is negative in 13 of 15 pools; costs fall per dollar as the position grows (table: $5,000).
- **TVL.** Spearman ρ between TVL and LP vs HODL = 0.22; between TVL and nominal APR =
  0.20.
- **Idle liquidity.** 7 of 15 pools had a median daily nominal APR below 0.5%: large reserves, almost no
  trading (#5462 wNEAR/chill-129, #6065 wNEAR/ZEC, #79 wNEAR/REF, #4771 wNEAR/touched, #535 wNEAR/stNEAR, #6679 wNEAR/itlx, #5650 wNEAR/purge-558). TVL there is capital parked, not capital earning.
- **Burrow.** 7 of 18 markets accrued a supply yield more than
  0.5 pp away from the APR the contract reports today. This is the weakest count: in most markets (11 of 18) the displayed rate is close to what accrued; the gap is concentrated in the markets listed first in the Burrow table.

## Method

- **Data.** One mainnet block per day, the block closest to 00:00 UTC (within 10 minutes), read through an
  archival RPC. View calls only: `get_pool`, `get_pool_volumes` and `metadata` on `v2.ref-finance.near`;
  `get_assets_paged_detailed` on `contract.main.burrow.near`; `gas_price`. The Rhea answers are stored verbatim. For
  Burrow, a projection of the listed fields plus the sha256 of the full answer is stored.
- **Pricing.** Each Rhea pool is valued in NEAR at its own price (wNEAR reserve / other reserve), so no external
  price enters any yield figure. For a constant-product pool TVL = 2 × wNEAR reserve. NEAR/USD is used only to size the
  $500 and $5,000 positions and to state TVL in USD. It is the mean of DefiLlama (coingecko:near) and the Coinbase
  5-minute candle when they agree within 150 bps; 90 of 91 days are priced.
  End price: $4.0049.
- **Nominal APR (24h)** = gross fees of the day (cumulative input volumes × `total_fee`) / TVL × 365. This is the
  usual "24h fee APR" of a pool list. The number in Rhea's own interface was not available to compare against.
- **Fee APR realized** = the LP share of fees over the window (gross × (1 − `admin_fee_bps`)) / mean TVL, annualised.
- **LP vs HODL** = value of one LP share at the end / value, at the end price, of the tokens that share held at the
  start − 1. This is measured from `shares_total_supply`, not modelled. It contains fees and impermanent loss
  together. The IL column is the constant-product formula on the window's price change, for reference.
- **Net APR ($S)** = LP vs HODL annualised − (round-trip cost of a $S position / $S) × 365 / window days. The round trip
  is: swap half in, then out again through the pool at the end reserves (fees and price impact), plus 4 × 180 Tgas of
  attached gas at the end gas price (100000000 yocto/gas): an upper bound, refunds not counted.
- **Burrow realized supply APR** = growth of the supply share price (supplied balance / supplied shares) over the
  window, annualised. It is compared with the `supply_apr` the contract reports at the end block.
- **Annualisation** is simple (× 365 / days), the same for every column.

## Counterarguments, and what the data says

1. **"A 24h figure is noisy: average it."** The daily nominal APR is given as a range across the window, and the
   realized fee APR is that average, measured (the LP share, after the admin fee). The gap to LP vs HODL remains, because the average still leaves
   out impermanent loss.
2. **"Impermanent loss is temporary."** It is temporary only if the price returns. The 30-day column next to the
   90-day one shows how much the result depends on the window. LP vs HODL is what an LP who entered at
   the start and left at the end actually had.
3. **"A pool's own price can be manipulated."** These are daily points, not a single trade. As a check, pool #4512
   (wNEAR/USDC) priced NEAR within a median 16 bps
   (max 78 bps) of the external two-source NEAR/USD across
   90 daily points.
4. **"Farms and boosts pay LPs and lenders more."** They do, and they are excluded here on both sides: neither Rhea farm
   rewards nor Burrow incentives are counted. A displayed APR that includes rewards would sit further above the
   realized base yield, and the reward token's own price risk would come on top.
5. **"The gas cost is overstated."** It is an upper bound. For $5,000 the cost per dollar is small. The $500 column
   shows where costs matter, and the cost figures are in `pools.csv`.
6. **"The sample is biased."** The universe is the top 15 wNEAR pools by reserve at the END block. That is
   survivorship: pools that drained during the window are not in it, which flatters LP outcomes. So the gap is, if
   anything, understated.
7. **"One quarter is one market regime."** Yes. Over these 90 days NEAR moved from
   1.658 to 4.012 USDC (pool #4512), and the impermanent loss of NEAR pairs follows that move. Another quarter can give other numbers. The claim
   is about what the displayed figures leave out, not about a fixed number.
8. **"HODL is the wrong benchmark: compare with simply holding NEAR."** In NEAR terms an LP share of these pools
   returned a median -42.7% a year (`lp_return_in_near_apr` in `pools.csv`):
   in a quarter when NEAR rose, a pool sells NEAR on the way up and ends with less of it. Measured against NEAR alone,
   11 of 15 pools did worse than against HODL. Neither benchmark
   closes the gap to the displayed APR, which counts no price risk at all.

## Limitations

- Daily granularity; simple annualisation; constant-product (v1 SIMPLE_POOL) pools only. Concentrated-liquidity (DCL)
  and stable pools are not modelled.
- Rewards and incentives are excluded (counterargument 4).
- Burrow balances are in the contract's inner units; the share-price ratio is unit-free, so this does not affect the
  realized APR.
- The round-trip cost ignores the price move between entry and exit, which is inside LP vs HODL already.
- The NEAR/USD mean of two sources has the same quorum rule as the live system. Days without agreement stay unpriced.
